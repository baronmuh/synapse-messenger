# PART A — VISION (former `VISION.txt`)

# Project: Synapse — Vision for AI Agent Organization Infrastructure (Vision and Design Document)

## V.1. Status and Scope

This document is a **vision and design spec**: it describes Synapse's
product direction and the evolution that must transform it from a local
messaging system for AI agents into a **communication and coordination
infrastructure for organizations of AI agents**. It formed, with the former
`T.txt`, two complementary documents; since the 2026-08-05 merge, it
constitutes **Part A** of this unified document (`SPEC.txt`), whose Part B
is the API v2 specification, source of truth — which it guides.

Reading rules:

* each feature is presented with the problem solved, the solution
  proposed, the integration into the existing codebase, and the estimated
  complexity;
* horizons are: short term (🟢), medium term (🟡), long term (🔴), and
  vision (🚀);
* decisions recorded (section V.6) are those validated by the user;
  they will not be revisited during design;
* no feature in this document is yet implemented; each selected evolution
  will be the subject of a detailed spec (in the style of existing
  specification documents) and then an amendment to Part B (`SPEC.txt`)
  before implementation.

## V.2. Product Vision

Synapse is not a messaging system between coding agents. It is an
**effective communication infrastructure between AI agents**, enabling a
set of specialized agents to collaborate to accomplish complex tasks
**in any domain**.

Communication is not simply "Agent A → message → Agent B" but:
"Agent A → objective → coordination → multiple agents → results →
synthesis".

Ultimately, Synapse must enable the creation of **networks of agents
capable of functioning like a real organization**: an HR agent, employee
agents, a manager agent, an accounting agent, a sales agent, a marketing
agent, a legal agent, a support agent, an operations manager, specialized
agents. These agents communicate, transmit information, request actions,
provide reports, collaborate on tasks, and make decisions collectively.

And beyond: a **platform enabling humans to create, manage, and observe
entire organizations of AI agents**, as one would manage a company — via
a visualization interface (dashboard) built on top of Synapse, without
ever modifying the server's architecture.

## V.3. Positioning and Guiding Principles

Synapse sits on the "agent ↔ agent" layer (equivalent to the A2A protocol
of the Linux Foundation: capability discovery, delegation, task
coordination) with guarantees that this layer generally does not offer:
organizations, strong per-command authentication, fine-grained permissions,
communication policies, non-disclosure, verified persistence, audit. The
"local-first, secure, sovereign" niche is an advantage, not a limitation.

Guiding principles, applicable to any evolution:

1. **Non-regression**: the 26 final constraints of Part B remain in
   force, unless explicitly amended and recorded in this document (section V.6);
2. **security and compliance before performance**: no optimization
   sacrifices non-disclosure, immutability, authentication, or
   persistence;
3. **small first**: any feature must work in a minimal organization of
   two agents without additional configuration (section V.4);
4. **real problem first**: no feature without an identified use case;
   no complex architecture without measurable justification;
5. **each new command** is authenticated, strictly validated,
   documented in `help()` (anti-drift) and covered by tests;
6. **all new data** is persisted, included in backups and restorations,
   and protected by the same non-disclosure rules as the existing;
7. **pull and database state take precedence**: no asynchronous processes
   while consultable events (F10) suffice; tasks and states are durable
   (in the database, never in memory);
8. **start simple**: fixed roles and permissions first, never a
   customizable rules engine from the start (complexity risk).

## V.4. Organizations of All Sizes — The "Small First" Principle

A Synapse organization does not necessarily represent a company.
It represents **a group of agents under a common authority**. Sizes
and forms are multiple:

* **two coordinated agents**: two agents performing two different tasks
  and communicating to better coordinate (typical example);
* **a team**: three to ten agents around a common goal;
* **a company**: departments, teams, roles, hundreds of agents;
* **an ecosystem**: several organizations connected (partners,
  suppliers) — vision horizon (F20).

Rules:

1. the current foundation of Part B — flat organization (agents +
   communication policy) — remains **the valid minimal model**; nothing
   should force it to evolve;
2. no feature can **require** hierarchy (department, team, role):
   tasks (F5), work queues (F6), events (F10), and audit (F11) work
   fully in a flat organization of two agents;
3. hierarchy (F13) is an **organizational option**, never a
   precondition; an organization that does not need it does not create one;
4. examples in this document alternate scales: the case "two agents
   coordinating their tasks" is as legitimate as the case "company of a
   hundred agents".

## V.5. Infrastructure Pillars

Synapse's evolution is organized into seven complementary pillars:

* **A. Discovery and Directory** — knowing who does what (cards, search,
  structure);
* **B. Task-Oriented Coordination** — delegate, follow, validate, escalate
  (the core);
* **C. Advanced Communication** — events, transfers, groups;
* **D. Identity and Capabilities** — role, skills, limits, reputation;
* **E. Governance and Security** — permissions, controlled delegation, audit;
* **F. Observability** — who talked to whom, where are the blockages;
* **G. Interface and Scale** — dashboard, volume, interoperability.

Sections 8 to 11 detail features by horizon. Each sheet indicates the
concerned pillar.

## V.6. Decisions Recorded (from Analysis)

The following decisions were validated by the user. They constrain the
detailed specs to come.

### V.6.1 Authentication Verification Cache — APPROVED (yes)

Server throughput is capped by Argon2id verification (~255 ms and 64 MiB
per command; ~9.5 req/s measured on the reference machine). The cache
temporarily remembers (TTL 30 to 60 seconds) the result of a successful
authentication per principal; failures are never cached; the cache is
invalidated on any hash rotation (password change).

Impact: throughput multiplied by 3 to 10 for agents that chain commands.
This is the **prerequisite for the entire vision**: without it, an
organization of a hundred active agents suffocates.

Compliance: authentication remains verified in each command (Part B
constraint 2); the cache remembers the result of this verification. The
promise "each command pays the Argon2id computation" is formally amended
in Part B during implementation. The complete design is documented in
`docs/PERFORMANCE.md` §6.

### V.6.2 Next Structuring Step: Tasks — APPROVED (yes)

The next major evolution is the **task system** (F5), rather than
discussion groups or fine-grained permissions. Tasks meet the real need
for coordination ("do X by such date", with tracking, transfer, and
history) at the right level of abstraction. Group conversations (F15)
and granular permissions (F14) will come after.

### V.6.3 Organizational Structure: Start Simple — APPROVED (yes)

Hierarchy (departments, teams, roles) will be introduced with **fixed
predefined roles** (for example: `manager`, `employee`, `rh`), not a
customizable permissions engine. The complete system can be enriched
later; complexity is contained at the start.

### V.6.4 Web Interface: Observer Account via Server — APPROVED (yes)

The future web interface (F18) will access data **through the server**,
like a read-only principal (observer account), never by direct reading
of database files. Single-process architecture and consistency are
preserved.

### V.6.5 Federation: Standard A2A Gateway — APPROVED (yes)

Multi-server binding (F20) will rely on the **A2A standard** (Linux
Foundation), not a proprietary protocol. Synapse will expose a compatible
gateway; it will not be isolated from the rest of the agent world.
